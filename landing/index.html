Capabilities

Everything a product record needs

Not a document dump with a parts list bolted on — a real product data model with the rules enforced on the server.

Product structure

◫

Parts & revisions

Lettered revisions with a lifecycle state machine — In Work, In Review, Released, Obsolete. Released revisions are immutable, enforced server-side, not by convention.

⌗

Multi-level eBOM

Find numbers, quantities, units, reference designators, an indented structure tree, cycle prevention and where-used. Children resolve to their latest released revision — and a child that has none is shown at its working revision and flagged unreleased, rather than quietly omitted.

⇄

BOM compare & baselines

Diff any two revisions — or two entirely different products — as one aligned tree. Freeze a structure as a named baseline and compare baselines to each other.

◇

Effectivity, alternates, variants

Date-effective BOM lines with an “as of” view, substitute parts per line, and option-driven variants resolved by a configurator.

≡

Requirements traceability

Typed requirements with decomposition, linked to the parts that satisfy them and the documents that verify them, plus a coverage matrix.

Documents & CAD

◈

Versioned documents

Every upload is a new version, never an overwrite, linked to the parts, revisions and changes it belongs to. The whole history stays downloadable.

⊙

The vault: check out, check in

Take the lock before you edit and nobody else can upload over you. Locks show who holds them and expire on their own, so a colleague on holiday never blocks the drawing.

◉

3D CAD in the browser

STEP, IGES and BREP tessellated server-side by an OpenCascade kernel, plus STL, glTF, OBJ and 3MF — orbit and inspect an assembly with no plugin and no desktop licence.

✎

Redline markup & review

Pin a comment to a point on a 3D model or an image and thread the discussion there; PDFs and other formats take document-level notes. When a redline turns out to be a real change, escalate it into a change request that carries the whole thread with it.

◫

cBOM from CAD

Read the assembly structure straight out of a STEP file, match products to parts, and reconcile all three views: what was modelled, what engineering released, and what manufacturing consumes.

Manufacturing & the floor

⚙

Process plans (mBOM)

Per-revision routings: sequenced operations with work centers and setup/run times, each consuming materials — the mBOM alongside the engineering one, reconciled against it.

⬡

Materials & what to buy

Declare the stock a machined part draws — alloy, form, net quantity, scrap factor. Explode a build of any quantity into gross material totals and estimated cost, with the parts that declare no material listed just as loudly, because those are the holes you would otherwise order against.

⌗

Serial & lot traceability

Record what was actually built, not just what was designed. Ask which shipped serial numbers contain a suspect lot and get the list, with the chain of consumptions that put it there — plus an as-built versus as-designed report showing every substitution and unplanned draw.

⚒

Service & as-maintained

A unit in the field is not the unit you shipped. Book a repair against a serial number, record the part that came out and the one that went in, and the genealogy updates — so the as-maintained structure stays true years after release.

⌸

Quality: NCR & CAPA

Nonconformances with severity, quantity and disposition that escalate straight into a change, and CAPAs whose Verify step refuses to advance without a root cause and a corrective action, and whose Close step refuses while any linked nonconformance is still open.

Change, approval & access

⟳

Engineering change control

Requests triaged into change notices, affected items with from → to revisions and stock dispositions, effectivity by date or by serial number, and a printable notice for the floor. The rules it enforces →

✓

Electronic signatures

Signature requirements per role or named signer, password re-entry at the moment of signing, and a printable record carrying printed name, meaning and timestamp. The authentication method is recorded on every signature, so the strength of each one stays auditable. Change what a signer attested to — the affected items, their revisions, their dispositions — and the signature voids itself.

⊘

Item-level access control

Restrict an individual part, document, change, project or build unit to named people and groups. A restricted item answers exactly like one that does not exist — absent from lists, absent from search, and a plain 404 on a direct hit, because a “forbidden” reply already tells an outsider it is there.

◐

Redaction that keeps the maths honest

A restricted child on a BOM you can read stays as a Restricted row with its quantity intact — never silently dropped. Roll-ups say so when a total could not be completed, so no one mistakes a censored number for a small one.

▤

Projects & phase gates

Projects with an ordered gate set and deliverables bound to parts, documents, requirements or changes. A gate refuses to pass while an earlier gate is open or a required deliverable is outstanding.

Sourcing & cost

Cost roll-up

Unit costs rolled through the multi-level BOM, with the parts whose cost is unknown named rather than quietly counted as zero.

⌾

AML & supplier RFQ

Approved manufacturer lists per part, then RFQs with quote comparison, lead time, MOQ, automatic lowest-price flagging and award tracking.

⇄

Supplier portal

Invite a supplier to quote and they get their own scoped login. They see the lines and their own prices — never a competitor's price, never who won, never an RFQ they weren't invited to.

↧

Vendor catalog import

Drop in a distributor's CSV export — Digi-Key, Mouser, Farnell/Newark and RS Components are recognised from their column headers alone — map the columns once, and see every row classified as new, an update or a problem before anything is written. A careless import is how a part master fills with duplicates.

The difference

Change control that actually holds

Most lightweight tools let you edit a BOM and call it a day. TurboPLM treats a change as a governed object, because that is the part auditors, customers and your own future self care about.

A complete change chain

  • Requests (ECR) raised against a part, triaged and accepted into a change notice
  • Change notices (ECN) carrying affected items and from → to revisions
  • Effectivity by date or by serial number — “from unit 0042 onward” is how a factory actually cuts a change in
  • Stock disposition per part: use as is, rework, scrap, return to vendor
  • A printable change notice for the people on the shop floor

Rules the server enforces

  • A part can sit on only one active change at a time
  • Revisions under a change cannot be released behind its back
  • Releasing a change releases every one of its revisions atomically
  • Nothing releases while a child part is still unreleased
  • Mutations through the internal API land in an audit trail with who, what and when

Approvals that fit your process

  • Flat reviewer sign-off, or a configurable multi-step workflow
  • Steps that need any one approver, or all of them
  • Assignees by named user or by role
  • Tasks queued in each reviewer’s inbox, with notifications

Know the blast radius first

  • Where-used rollup across every affected part before you approve
  • Side-by-side BOM diff of exactly what the change does
  • Requirement coverage so you see what a change puts at risk
  • Analytics on change cycle time and BOM health
Need to know

Not everyone should see everything

A customer programme under NDA, an export-controlled assembly, an acquisition still unannounced — most PLM either gives everyone the whole database or forces you into a second system. TurboPLM lets you close a single item without splitting your product record in two.

Closed means invisible, not scolded

  • Grant a part, document, change, project or build unit to named people and groups
  • A restricted item is absent from lists, absent from search and absent from exports
  • A direct hit answers 404, byte for byte the same as an item that never existed — a “forbidden” reply would confirm it is there
  • Read access and write access are separate grants, so a reviewer can be let in without being let loose

Opt-in, so nothing changes until you say so

  • An item with no grants behaves exactly as before — deploying this locks nobody out
  • The first grant is what closes an item, and the interface says so before you add it
  • Group membership is resolved at query time; deactivating a group suspends every grant it holds at once
  • Administrators always retain a way back in, so an item cannot be orphaned

Hidden without lying

  • A restricted child on a BOM you can read stays visible as a Restricted row with its quantity intact
  • Cost and material roll-ups that crossed something hidden say so, rather than returning a smaller number that looks complete
  • A recall trace still walks through restricted assemblies, so the shipped list is never quietly short
  • Where-used still reports that something you cannot see consumes this part — the honest answer to “is it safe to change?”

Verified, not asserted

  • The test suite enumerates the server's own GET route table at runtime and drives each route against a restricted item
  • A read endpoint added tomorrow is swept the moment it is registered — and the sweep fails if it cannot address a route, rather than scoring it as covered
  • Access is resolved in one place in the codebase — no route re-implements the rules
  • Machine credentials are subject to the same filter; an API key never outranks the person who created it

Self-hosted by design

Your product data stays yours

Product structures, costs and supplier terms are among the most sensitive data a hardware company has. TurboPLM is built to run where you decide.

No tenant, no metering

Run it on a laptop, a NAS or a VM. No seat counts, no usage telemetry, no per-user tax on inviting a colleague.

Plain Postgres

Your data sits in a database you can query, dump and back up yourself — no proprietary store, no export fee.

Open integration

Scoped API keys, signed webhooks, ERP-shaped item and BOM export, CSV import that shows you what will change before it writes.

Readable source

TypeScript end to end, a documented contract between the API and the client, and a schema you can extend.

Download and run it yourself

Clone the repository and bring the whole stack up with Docker Compose — the application, the database and the CAD service, seeded with demo data, in a couple of minutes.

Open source under the GNU AGPL v3: read it, run it, change it, and use it for anything at all. The one obligation is reciprocal — if you modify TurboPLM and offer it to others over a network, your changes have to be published too.
